# WP-142 — slip_reduce: the memory bound is checked by what reduce holds, not by resident pages

This file is the inventory of item 1 of `docs/wp/WP-142.md`: everything
`crates/slip_reduce/src/` holds in memory whose size is a function of the
number of shards, of runs, or of observers, with its cost at 10,000 and at
1,000,000 shards. (The package names `spec/landed/reduce/`; there is no
spec `reduce.md`, and `cargo xtask lint` takes a landed file only under an
existing spec, so it is under `edits`, whose §5.6 it is about.)

Sizes are of a 64-bit build: a `Digest` is 32 bytes, a `Vec` that grows by
pushes has a capacity of up to twice its length. "Runs" are the first
pass's runs, `ceil(shards / fan_in)`.

## 1. Held per observer, per shard or per run

| # | What (where) | Grows with | At 10,000 shards | At 1,000,000 shards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The unparsed observers, `Vec<Digest>` (`lib.rs`, `basis_of`), which become `EditSet.unparsed` (§6: `list<digest>`) | unparsed observers | 32 B each: 312 KiB if all are unparsed (capacity up to 625 KiB) | 30.5 MiB (capacity up to 61 MiB) |
| 2 | The runs' joined states waiting to be folded in run order, `BTreeMap<usize, Joined>` (`lib.rs`, `run`, `folding`) | runs that finish before an earlier one; with one thread at most one | `fan_in` 64: up to 156 states; `fan_in` 2: up to 4,999 | `fan_in` 64: up to 15,624; `fan_in` 2: up to 499,999 |
| 3 | The number of files of each merge level, `Cleanup::levels: Vec<usize>` (`runs.rs`) | `log_fan_in(runs)` | `fan_in` 2: 14 levels, 112 B | `fan_in` 2: 20 levels, 160 B |

Item 2 needs several threads: the pool hands out runs in order, and a run
that is slow (one large shard) while the other threads finish the runs
after it leaves every one of those runs' `Joined` in the map until it
finishes. A `Joined` is empty maps (about 170 B) plus that run's joined
subjects (a `SubjectJoin` holds five `Sample`s, a rule and a display: a few
hundred bytes each), links, rules, ties, and one entry per shard counter
name (`api.*`, `edits.*`: eleven or more). For a rename's run, about 1 to
3 KiB: at 1,000,000 shards with `fan_in` 64, up to 15 to 45 MiB in the
worst case. It is not reached with one thread, and it is not reached when
the runs finish in about their order, which is the usual case.

Nothing else grows with the shards, the runs or the observers:

- The plan's "one observer once" check is not a set in memory: every run
  writes a record `Key::Observer` per index into its temporary file, the
  merges join them, and the final stream reads them one at a time
  (`lib.rs`, after the merge). Memory: `fan_in` of them in a run's map.
- The basis is a file of 65 bytes per observer (`runs.rs`), read
  sequentially; the second pass reads `fan_in` entries of it per run.
- A run holds its records (`BTreeMap<Key, Val>`, at most `fan_in` shards'
  collapsed records) and `fan_in` basis entries; a merge holds `fan_in`
  readers (one record and one 8 KiB buffer each), their paths, a heap of
  `fan_in` keys, and one writer. The pool keeps nothing per job.
- Every `Sample` (`first` of a claim, a report, a subject's `seen` and
  `flag_seen`, a failure) holds at most 8 indices.
- The second pass's union `seen` is one set per checked file, of the
  distinct `(size, content)` the shards gave it; the runs' parts are
  folded into it as they finish (a union, in any order).
- The test's `Lazy` store makes each shard when opened and holds nothing;
  its `observer(i)` is computed.

## 2. Held per distinct record of the transform

These grow with the transform (distinct sites, paths, records), not with
the observers or the claims: ten thousand observers that say one thing are
one record. They are listed because §5.6's last paragraph names only three
of them.

| What (`set.rs`, `Builder` unless said) | One entry per |
|---|---|
| `paths`, `versions` | path some shard's `edits.files` names; distinct `(size, content)` of it |
| `live` | live site (§5.4), with its pieces and `ClaimVal` |
| `members` | claimed site, with the ranges it copies |
| `blocking` | site with a kept or unresolved range |
| `kept`, `unresolved`, `refusals` | such site |
| `reports`, `changed`, `failures` | joined record |
| `Joined::subjects`, `links`, `rules` (`pass1.rs`) | joined subject, link, rule |
| `Joined::ties` (`groups.rs`) | distinct grouped edit |
| `Joined::counters` (`pass1.rs`) | distinct shard counter name `api.*`, `edits.*` |
| the selection (`subjects.rs`) | subject, link |
| the geometry's vectors (`geometry.rs`) | live site, claimed site, copied range |
| the returned `EditSet` | its records (§6) |

## 3. The resident readings of the gate

With one thread and every shard `ok` (the gate's test,
`one_site_ten_thousand_shards_fan_in_two`), items 1 and 2 of §1 are empty
and item 3 is 112 B against 80 B: nothing reduce holds explains about
300 KiB more for 10,000 shards than for 1,000. As far as reading can tell
the difference is the measurement's: on Linux 6.2 and later a process's
resident counters are per-CPU counters read approximately (up to a batch
of pages per CPU is not yet folded in, and a run that lasts 20 s instead
of 1.5 s migrates over more of the 24 CPUs), and the `VmHWM` the test reads
after `clear_refs` is already off from `VmRSS` by 200 KiB in the failing
log. The allocator's slack (the 8 KiB buffers of 15,000 temporary files
made and dropped in turn) and the scratch directory's entries (kernel
memory, not resident pages of the process) are the other candidates; the
reading cannot tell them apart.
