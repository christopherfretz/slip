# WP-087 — the keys a transform's declarations need

What `crates/slip_digest/src/query.rs` (`slip_digest::query::Keys`) reads
into `spec/api.md` §7.5, `spec/verify.md` §2.1, §2.3, §9.5 and
`spec/edits.md` §9.1, §9.2, §9.7 where the specs are silent, and the
stricter reading taken each time. The tests are
`crates/slip_digest/tests/api_keys.rs`, named after each decision.

## The interface, and its departures

As `docs/wp/WP-087.md` gives it, plus two refusals that only
`rename_rule` gives. They keep apart the two outcomes of `spec/api.md`
§7.5 rule 3:

- `NoKey::NotRenamable` (`unsupported`): the entity has a key, but it is
  not a unit keyed by name.
- `NoKey::BadName` (`invalid_argument`): the new name is not an
  identifier, or is the entity's own name.

The brief allowed a `NoKey` or an error of its own. Variants keep one
error type for the callers (WP-095, WP-071), and neither can be confused
with `NotAUnit`.

Also public, but hidden from the documentation: `query::walk_units` and
`query::WalkUnit`, the walk's own list of the units it writes. Each
entry has its entity, key, group class, whether it is a resolved or
`default_argument` unit, and the cause the walk's `Walk::key` gives its
key. It is the oracle of tests 1 and 3, and what `derived` lists from.

## Every answer is the walk's

`Keys` holds a `Walk` of its own and calls only the functions the walk
calls: `Walk::entity`, `Walk::key`, `Walk::key_bytes`,
`units::unit_class`, `local::unit_of`, `local::owner`,
`local::param_template(s)`. `derived` runs the walk itself
(`walk::run_with`) once per `Keys`, under the same rules. No key field
is derived a second way. The walk does not call `query.rs`, so no
digest depends on it. Outside `query.rs` the change is one `pub mod`
line in `lib.rs` and `pub(crate)` on `local::param_template` and
`local::param_templates`.

`new` and `with_rules` run nothing: no unit is collected and no owner is
numbered ahead of need. A local type's number is computed when first
asked for, and the memo is never sealed. Test 1 checks that this agrees
with the full walk (which numbers every owner first) on 25,336 units of
4,976 pair-test sides. Test `answers_do_not_depend_on_order` checks that
the answers do not depend on the order they are asked in.

## `key_id` and `key`

- **A key the walk computes with a cause** (`KeyRes::Key { cause:
  Some(_) }`: a discriminator or parent with a pending or unsupported
  area, such as a closure in a signature) is `Indeterminate(cause)`.
  It is not given: a key with a cause may not tell the entity apart
  from another (`a_key_with_a_cause_is_indeterminate`).
- **The rule test 1 checks.** When the walk's own `Walk::key` gives a
  unit's key with a cause (`WalkUnit::key_cause`, read from the walk's
  memo after it has written every unit), `key_id` must be
  `Indeterminate` with that same cause. `Ok`, even with the walk's key,
  is a failure. A content-keyed unit is `ContentKeyed` whatever its
  cause, since its key is its stream's digest.

  The mutation that lets a `Pending` cause through as `Ok` is equivalent
  on today's main, and the rule cannot kill it by construction: no key
  computation writes a `Pending` area. A type's `Pending(Spelling)` is
  absorbed only outside keys (`in_key == 0`), and the `Pending(Demand)`
  placeholders are in unit records, not keys. The rule kills that
  mutation as soon as a pending area reaches a key.
- **A local entity that is no local type** (`Walk::key` gives
  `key/local`) is `NotAUnit`, as a parameter is.
- **Keys of entities that are not units:** a local type, a member of a
  local class, or a specialisation that is only named have a key that
  some stream writes, so they get `Ok`. `unit_of` tells the caller
  whether the entity is a unit.
- **Collisions and taints are not seen.** A key that collides in the
  translation unit (digest §3.4) is still given. So is the key of a
  unit that a declaration without a key kind taints. The walk makes
  both units indeterminate, so whatever is declared about them
  compares unequal anyway (`spec/verify.md` §9.5).
- `key` reads `key_id` first, so the two refuse alike by construction.
  Then `key` rebuilds the key's bytes (`Walk::key_bytes`, the function
  the records read) and checks the id. A mismatch would be an internal
  defect and is `Indeterminate(query/key-again)`; it never occurs
  (test 8).

## `unit_of`

- **The innermost unit**, following `Walk::entity` and the semantic
  context. A default member initialiser is held by its field, since the
  field's stream holds it (digest-kinds §5.5 `FieldData`); digest §2.1
  allows the class or the field.
- **A local entity's unit** is `local::unit_of`'s: its owner, or the
  owner's unit when the owner is written in place. A closure in a
  namespace variable's initialiser is held by the variable, though its
  context is the translation unit.
- **A closure's static invoker** (`__invoke`) is implicit and has a
  body, so it is a derived unit even though its class is local
  (`local::derived_under_local`). It holds its own copy of the call
  operator's parameters.
- **A template parameter** is held by its template's unit
  (`local::param_template`; Kiln gives it the template's context).
- **No unit** (`None`): the translation unit, a lifetime-extended
  temporary, and a declaration whose chain of contexts reaches no unit.
- **The holder, not where the bytes are.** A closure in a default member
  initialiser of a field of an implicit instantiation (`TD<int>::m`) is
  held by the field. Its bytes are in the field's `<default_argument 0>`
  unit (digest-kinds §11.4), which `derived` of the field (and of the
  template) lists.
- **An implicit member nothing uses** (a closure's conversion function,
  an unused implicit constructor, an implicit deduction guide) is its own
  unit (`unit_class` is `Some`), though the walk never writes it.
  `unit_of` answers "which unit would hold it", not "which unit is
  written".

## `derived`

The brief's "its resolved properties, the instantiations of it and of
its members" is read as "what the walk writes as derived and reaches
from `e`" (the fix round's reading):

- **Reached** means reached from `e` by any chain of these edges, from
  origin to entity:
  - a specialisation from its template, and from the pattern it was
    instantiated from;
  - a member of a specialisation from the member it was instantiated
    from;
  - anything with Kiln's `instantiated_from`, from that;
  - a member from its class (semantic and lexical context);
  - a local type from its owner;
  - the holding variable of a tuple-like binding from the binding;
  - the unnamed object of an anonymous class from the class.

  No edge leaves a namespace for its members.
- **Listed:** the resolved and `default_argument` units of `e` that the
  walk writes, and every unit of a derived group that the walk writes
  whose entity is reached, with its resolved and `default_argument`
  units. Units are taken from the walk's own list (`walk_units`), so:
  - an on-demand unit is listed exactly when this translation unit
    refers to it. One example is a generic lambda's `operator()<int>`,
    derived from the function that owns the closure;
  - a unit the walk never writes is never listed;
  - a declared unit is never listed.
- **A class's implicit members** that the walk writes are derived from
  the class (`I::I()` of `I`, the copy constructor of a renamed class).
  This reverses the first round's reading, which listed instantiations
  only. Property (b) below requires it: an exemption on the class
  covers what changes with the class.
- **Properties checked on every pair-test side**
  (`keys_agree_with_the_walk_on_every_pair_test`):
  - (a) every key that `derived(d)` gives, for every declaration `d`,
    is the key of a unit the walk writes. 15,430 keys, no exception.
  - (b) every unit of the walk's derived groups (6,463) is listed by
    `derived(d)` for some `d` whose unit is a declared unit of the walk.

  Exceptions to (b), each tested by its reason:
  - 1,575 units of entities **the compiler declares**: no declaration
    of the source declares them, so there is no declared `d`. These
    are builtins (`__builtin_abs`, `__builtin_coro_frame`), the implicit
    global allocation functions, `std::align_val_t`, `__va_list_tag`
    and its members, and their `<exception_spec>` units.
  - 2 **template parameter objects**: every specialisation whose
    arguments name the object shares it, and none is its origin.
- **Cost.** The first call builds an index of origins and runs the walk
  over the translation unit once, as `digest` does. Later calls only
  traverse the index.
- **Under `with_rules`** the walk runs under the same table, so the
  keys are mapped. `rename_rule_undoes_a_rename` checks that the
  renamed entity's `derived` in the post-image equals the original's.

## `rename_rule`

- The rule is read from `key(e)`. Both parents are the key's parent
  (the same scope), `c_linkage` on both sides for an entity with C
  language linkage (verify §2.3, `c_linkage_rule_keeps_its_parent`).
  The kind and discriminator are the key's. `name` is
  `identifier(new_name)` and `to_name` is the key's name. On a
  `with_rules` `Keys` the parent is in mapped terms, which is what a
  table that already renames the enclosing scope needs.
- **`BadName`** (`invalid_argument`), checked in this order:
  - a `new_name` that is not `[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_]*`. This is judged
    from the text alone, before the entity, in the order §7.5 rule 3
    lists the outcomes, so a bad name for a content-keyed unit is
    still `BadName`;
  - after the entity's key is found, a `new_name` equal to its current
    name.
- **`NotRenamable`** (`unsupported`):
  - a key of kind `specialisation`, `partial_specialisation` or
    `decomposition`, whose name only follows its template or its first
    binding (the rules WP-103 forbids a table to contain);
  - a key whose name is not an identifier (an operator, a constructor,
    an unnamed class, the unnamed namespace).
- **Limits of the identifier check.** Both fail closed. The repair is
  for Kiln's lexer to decide what an identifier is, once `slip_kiln`
  exposes that.
  - A keyword (`class`, `and`) is accepted. The post-image cannot then
    parse, so nothing verifies.
  - An identifier with a universal character name (`a\u00e9`) or a
    code point outside ASCII (`é`) is refused as `BadName`, though C++
    allows it. `spec/api.md` §7.5 leaves non-ASCII text undecided
    (`unsupported`) for qualified names; for a new name, refusing is
    the stricter reading.

  `rename_rule_refusals` states each limit as it behaves today.
- **Followers.** An unnamed class named by a renamed variable is
  refused, and its follower rule is built from `key` as
  `spec/edits.md` §9.2 says (`rule_of_an_unnamed_class_from_its_key`).
  §9.2's sentence that a function template's specialisations "map with
  the subject" does not hold on today's main: a specialisation's key
  takes its template's unmapped name. It holds once WP-103 lands (see
  below). The review also found two renames that need followers §9.2
  does not list: a typedef that names an unnamed class, and the members
  of an anonymous struct (the anonymous class, its object and its
  indirect fields). These are for §9.2's package; `Keys` gives their
  keys.

## What waits for WP-103

On today's main, eight renames do not give equal digests (test 4). Tests
7 and 8 hold for them. The ignored tests are each marked
`pending WP-103`. In six of them a specialisation's key takes its
template's unmapped name:

- `pending_rename_class_template_with_instantiation`;
- `pending_rename_function_template_with_instantiation`;
- `pending_rename_variable_template_with_instantiation`;
- `pending_rename_function_template_with_explicit_specialisation`;
- `pending_rename_member_function_template` (of a class that is not a
  template);
- `pending_rename_class_template_with_deduction` (its implicit
  deduction guides).

In the other two:

- `pending_rename_member_of_class_template_with_instantiation`: the
  rule on `S::m` does not reach `S<int>::m`.
- `pending_rename_structured_binding`: the decomposition's key keeps
  its first binding's unmapped name.

A rule table that `RuleTable::refused` refuses (WP-103) is not handled
here yet, because WP-103 is not on main. The walk keys nothing under
such a table, and `Keys` must do the same.
