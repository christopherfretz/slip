# WP-103 — mapped keys under rules (`tests/rules.rs`)

The test suite of the mapping of `spec/digest.md` §3.4 and
`spec/verify.md` §2.1: `crates/slip_digest/tests/rules.rs`, one
directory per case under `crates/slip_digest/tests/rules/`, and the
resolver `crates/slip_digest/tests/common/rules.rs`. It found two defects
in the mapping, both fixed here; one of them made two different programs
compare equal. Its review (fix round 2) found more, fixed here where the
mapping can fix them ("Fix round 2") and left to WP-113 where it cannot.

## The form of a case

```
tests/rules/<group>/<case>/a.cpp       the pre-image, digested with no rule
tests/rules/<group>/<case>/b.cpp       the post-image, digested under the table
tests/rules/<group>/<case>/rules.txt   the table (absent: no rule)
tests/rules/<group>/<case>/expect.txt  empty: equal; else the differences
```

### `rules.txt`

One line per rule; `#` starts a comment line; blank lines are skipped.
Two forms:

```
relocate [<kind>] <pre-entity> => <post-parent> :: <name|=>
rule <kind> | <parent> | <name> | <disc> | <to_parent> | <to_name>
```

- `relocate` is the line of `spec/verify.md` §2.3: the entity of the
  pre-image named by its display name (`spec/verify.md` §6.1), optionally
  with its kind in brackets where two entities share a name, is now
  called `<name>` (`=`: the same) under the post-image parent named after
  `=>` (empty: the global namespace). The resolver turns it into the rule
  that undoes it, `Rule { parent: m(P'), kind: K.kind, name: N', disc:
  K.disc, to_parent: K.parent, to_name: K.name }`, where `K` is the
  pre-image key and `m(P')` the mapped id of the post-image parent.
- `rule` is the `Rule` itself, for tables no declared change makes (wrong,
  chained, matching nothing). A parent is `root`, `c_linkage`, `builtin`,
  `pre <entity>`, `post <entity>` (the unit's id with no rule) or `mapped
  <entity>` (the post-image entity's id under the whole table). A name is
  `id <identifier>`, `name-of pre <entity>` or `name-of post <entity>`. A
  discriminator is `none`, `disc-of pre <entity>` or `disc-of post
  <entity>` (the post-image discriminator mapped under the whole table).
  An entity is `[<kind>] <display name>`.
- `# vacuous` on a line of its own: the case is equal with its table and
  also without it (see "Checks of every case").

A `relocate` line's new parent is mapped under the rules of the lines
above it, as `spec/verify.md` §2.3 says `slip declare` maps it: a line
whose new parent another line relocates comes after that line
(`parents/mapped-parent`, equal; `parents/mapped-parent-reversed`, the
same two lines in the other order, differs). A `rule` line's `mapped
<entity>` and `disc-of post <entity>` are under the table of the lines
above it too.

### `expect.txt`

The reader of the pair tests (`spec/digest.md` §14.1, `tests/common`),
with the lines it does not know handed to the suite:

- No line: the case is equal (`judge_equal`), and no unit is hard
  indeterminate.
- Otherwise the case differs exactly as the lines say: `changed`,
  `removed`, `added`, `indeterminate <name>` (a unit with a collision or
  another hard cause; compared by every display name the key has on
  either side), and the derived lines, judged loosely unless `exact
  derived` is given. A key with a hard-indeterminate unit is left out of
  `changed`, `added` and `removed`.
- `use <n> via <v> direct <d>`, the counters of `spec/verify.md` §9.1 for
  the rule on line `n` of `rules.txt`. The walk does not count yet
  (WP-069), so the suite measures them: the table is rebuilt with the
  rule's `to_name` replaced by `slip probe <n>`; `via` is the number of
  records of B with the probe's produced key, `direct` the number of
  records with the rule's produced key `(to_parent, kind, to_name, disc)`
  under the probe table (an entity the post-image still has under the
  name the rule gives). **These are not §9.1's counters**, and WP-069
  builds the real ones: they count records, not entities (one entity
  with units in two groups would count twice; §9.1 counts entities), and
  `direct` counts every record with the produced key, including one
  another rule produced (§9.1's `direct` counts the entities the walk
  reaches under the old name with no rule applied).
- `collisions <n>`: the post-image's counter `digest.collisions` is `n`
  (without the line, 0).
- `refused <text>`: the table cannot be made; the resolver's or
  `RuleTable::new`'s error contains `<text>`. Nothing else is checked.
- `# pending`: skipped and listed, as elsewhere.

### Checks of every case

- B digested twice under the table gives the same `Output` (item 11).
  Round 1 also digested B under the table of the reversed lines and
  compared the ids; that tested nothing, because `RuleTable::new` sorts
  its rules (the two tables are one value), and under §2.3 the order of
  the lines is not free. It is removed; the counter `digest.collisions`
  is now checked in every case (`collisions <n>`), which is a check of
  the walk;
- `records` of B under the table succeeds (its own check that each
  unit's key, computed again, has the unit's id);
- vacuity: an equal case with a non-empty table fails if B with no rule
  is already equal to A, unless `rules.txt` says `# vacuous`.

`SLIP_DIGEST_ONLY=<prefix>` narrows the run, `SLIP_DIGEST_DUMP=1` prints
both sides' units; `rules_txt_forms` tests the reader.

## Defects found and fixed

### 1. A collision among keys no unit has was not seen (item 9)

`units::mark_collisions` looks at written units only. An entity without
a unit is still written into references by its mapped key, so a table
that gives it another entity's key made a reference to one read as a
reference to the other, with no finding. Two different programs compared
equal:

- `wrong/named-only-specialisations`: the rule `rule specialisation |
  post W2 | id W2 | disc-of post W3<A> | post W1 | id W1` gave the only
  named `W2<A>` the key of `W1<A>`: `g(W2<A>*)` got the key and digest of
  `g(W1<A>*)`. Now `indeterminate <order>`.
- `wrong/merged-local-extern`: `relocate legacy => :: acme` while
  `legacy` still exists, a table `slip declare` makes. A block-scope
  `extern int hidden();` in a templated function in `acme` (no unit,
  digest §2.2) got the key of `legacy::hidden`. Now `indeterminate
  legacy::hidden()`.

Fix: `keys::mark_mapped_collisions`, called from `walk::run_with` after
`units::mark_collisions`. Under a non-empty table, the walk's key memo is
grouped by mapped id; in each group of two or more that is not all
namespaces, the entities are classed by their id under no rule (a
second, plain `Walk`); if there are two classes or more and one of them
has no unit with the key, every unit with the key is marked `Collision`,
or the order unit if none has it. The count of such keys joins
`collisions`. With no rule it does nothing. The call in `walk.rs` is
outside the files listed for the package: it is the one line that joins
the check to the walk's count of collisions.

### 2. A renamed template left its specialisations under the old name

A specialisation's key is (its template's key, the template's name,
its arguments). The parent was mapped, the name was not: under a rule
that renames a class, function or variable template, every
specialisation got a key A does not have (`kinds/class_template-renamed`
and the other `-renamed` template cases were false differences).

Fix: in `spec_key`, the name is the template's mapped name
(`Walk::mapped_name`: the rule table applied to the template's computed
key). With no rule the name is unchanged, so no key changes; this
changes how a key maps, not what a key is.

## Fix round 2

The review's findings, by its numbering.

### F1. Linkage and visibility from the enclosing namespaces (WP-113)

A member's unit holds neither the linkage it takes from the unnamed
namespace nor the visibility it takes from a namespace's attribute, so a
relocation that changes either compares equal while the object code
differs. `spec/verify.md` §2.1 now says so, narrows the soundness
argument to what the units hold, and names WP-113 as the package that
closes it. The cases, pending (`# pending`, `# needs: WP-113`), each with
the symbols clang 19 gives in its comment:

| Case | Symbol before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `needs-wp113/class-out-of-unnamed` | `t _ZN12_GLOBAL__N_14Impl1fEv` | `W _ZN4Impl1fEv` |
| `needs-wp113/inline-function-out-of-unnamed` | `t _ZN12_GLOBAL__N_16helperEv` | `W _Z6helperv` |
| `needs-wp113/variable-into-unnamed` | `B _ZN6detail7counterE` | `b _ZN12_GLOBAL__N_17counterE` |
| `needs-wp113/across-visibility` | `GLOBAL HIDDEN _ZN1h1fEv` | `GLOBAL DEFAULT _ZN1d1fEv` |

All four compare equal today; each lists the `changed` lines it should
give once the property is in the member's unit.
(`variable-into-unnamed` keeps an empty `namespace { }` in the
pre-image, so that the only difference is the move.)

The form a rule of kind `namespace` makes, renaming the unnamed
namespace to a named one, a named one to it, or merging one onto it, is
refused now: `Rule::validate` returns `RULE_UNNAMED_NAMESPACE` when a
namespace rule's `name` and `to_name` differ in being `Name::Anonymous`
(`wrong/unnamed-namespace-renamed-to-named`,
`named-namespace-renamed-to-unnamed`, `named-namespace-merged-onto-unnamed`,
all `refused`; all three compared equal before, with `d`/`D` symbols
swapped). Moving a member in or out (`relocate (anonymous
namespace)::X => :: =`) is a rule of the member's kind and cannot be told
apart by the rule: it stays for WP-113.

### F2. No rule matches a specialisation

`Rule::validate` refuses a rule of kind `specialisation` or
`partial_specialisation` (`RULE_ON_SPECIALISATION`). The place: it is the
one function every table passes through, both `RuleTable::new` (the
resolver, `slip declare`) and `RuleTable::from_sorted` (the reader of
the declared container, `slip_model/src/declared.rs`), so no table from
any source holds one, and the error reaches each caller as its own
(`RuleError::InvalidName`, `SortError::Invalid`). Refusing in the walk
instead would let a container carry a rule that silently matches
nothing. A specialisation's key follows its template's (defect 2), so
there is nothing for such a rule to do that is not a wrong equal:
`wrong/named-only-spec-reparented-defined-templates` (the review's
case: `g(W2<A>*)` given the key of `g(W1<A>*)`, equal before),
`named-only-spec-reparented-relocate-form` (the same from a `relocate`
line), and `wrong/named-only-specialisations` (defect 1's case, now
refused before it is digested). `slip_model`'s unit test
`refused_rules` covers both doors and the rules that stay allowed.

### F3. Members of instantiations follow the pattern member

`relocate S::f() => S :: g` left `S<int>::g` under its plain name, so
every use of it was a false difference. Now `compute_key` gives a member
instantiated from a pattern member (Kiln's `instantiated_from`: the
member link of a member of a specialisation, or the sparse map for a
member template's instantiation) the pattern member's mapped name, with
`Walk::mapped_name`, which recurses through the chain. A change of
`crates/slip_digest/src/keys.rs` only (`instantiated_pattern`,
`follow_pattern`).

- `instantiated/member-of-class-template-renamed` (the review's case),
  `nested-class-template-member` (`O<int>::I<char>::g` through two
  patterns), `swap-member-templates-call-follows`: equal.
- `swap-member-templates-of-class-template`, the review's other case:
  `changed u()`, a true difference: the post-image's `u()` calls `p`,
  which the rules say was `q`, while the pre-image's calls `p`; the
  program returns 2 where it returned 1. Before the fix the pattern
  members and the instantiations differed as well.
- A member of an explicit specialisation of the class has no pattern
  link: it is its own entity
  (`explicit-class-specialisation-member-does-not-follow`, equal with it
  unrenamed; `-needs-own-rule`, renamed with only the pattern's rule:
  differs; `-own-rule`: equal). An explicit specialisation of a member
  (`template <> int S<int>::f()`) has the link and follows
  (`explicit-member-specialisation-follows`): it is spelled with the
  pattern member's name, as a specialisation is with its template's.
- A rule written for the member of the implicit instantiation itself
  (decision: the stricter reading): if any rule matches the member's
  key, under its plain or its followed name, its key is indeterminate
  (`key/rule-on-instantiated-member`). No source renames an
  instantiation's member apart from its pattern's, so such a rule is a
  mistake whether or not the pattern's rule beside it makes it
  redundant (`direct-rule-beside-pattern-rule`, `direct-rule-alone`).
- Collisions are still caught (`instantiated/member-collision`: the
  pattern and the instantiation both collide, `collisions 2`).

### F4. A decomposition follows its first binding

lists the three keys that take another entity's mapped name, and the
names that stay plain: `unnamed_by`, an unnamed field or variable named

### The parent of a `relocate` line

The suite's resolver now maps a line's new parent under the lines above
it (§2.3). `parents/mapped-parent` (`relocate a => :: x` first, then
`relocate a::b => x :: y`): equal. `parents/mapped-parent-reversed` (the
same lines in the other order): the second line's parent `x` is not yet
mapped, its rule matches nothing, and `a::b` and its members show as
`removed`/`added`. That `slip declare` should refuse the wrong order
(a line whose new parent a later line relocates) is for its package, not
this one. §2.1's wording of `map(parent')` now says the same as §2.3.

A type digest memoised under one table and reused under another: no code
can do it. The memos (`type_memo`, `type_memo_no_exc`, `keys`, `content`,
`const_memo`) are fields of `Walk`, and a `Walk` is made for one
`Options`, whose `rules` it holds for its life (`walk::run_with`); there
is no memo outside a walk and none shared between walks. The suite would
see such a reuse anyway: every case digests B under its table after A
was digested under none, and twice more under the table and its reverse,
in one process.

## What the spec sections now say

- `spec/digest.md` §3.4: a key whose name is another entity's takes that
  entity's mapped name (defect 2); a collision is counted over every
  entity the walk keys, units or not, the order unit marked if no unit
  has the key, and entities equal under no rule are one (defect 1).
- `spec/verify.md` §2.1: `unnamed_by` holds the plain name, so a renamed
  typedef needs a rule for its class too (item 7); `φ` ranges over every
  keyed entity, not units only; a dependent name is written as a name,
  so a template nothing instantiates is outside what is shown.

## Open questions

- `slip declare` maps a `relocate` line's parent under the lines above
  only, the suite under the whole table: for a table whose lines name a
  parent before its own rule, the two differ. Which is the reading of
  §2.3?
- A decomposition is named by its first binding's plain name, and a
  using-declaration and its shadows by their own names: renaming a
  binding, or a member a using-declaration names, needs extra rules
  (`kinds/binding-renamed`, `using_declaration-renamed`). Should the
  mapping derive them, as it now does for specialisations?
- The counters are the suite's own until the walk counts (WP-069): the
  definition used here is in "`expect.txt`" above, for that package to
  check against.
- Item 11's other half, a walk in another process, is not run under
  rules: `tests/determinism.rs` is outside this package's files.
- `judge_equal`'s "differs" message names units whose digests are equal
  under rules, because its comparison includes the display name; the
  verdict is right, the message misleading.
