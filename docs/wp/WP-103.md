# WP-103 — slip_digest: mapped keys under rules (`tests/rules.rs`)

- Status: in-progress
- Milestone: M1
- Track: C
- Needs: WP-039, WP-055
- Size: M
- Parallel with: WP-040, WP-041, WP-045, WP-046, WP-049, WP-104, WP-105

## Scope

Verification digests the post-image under the rules that undo the
declared relocations and compares for plain equality (`spec/digest.md`
§3.4, `spec/verify.md` §2.1). Everything a namespace migration is
verified through rests on that mapping, and today it has two unit tests
(`crates/slip_digest/src/tests.rs`, a renamed top-level namespace) and
the handful of snippets `slip declare` is tested on
(`docs/wp/WP-055.md`: "the tests of `declare` here do not stand in for
`tests/rules.rs`"). This package is the mapping's own test suite:
`crates/slip_digest/tests/rules.rs` and one directory per case under
`crates/slip_digest/tests/rules/`.

This is a package of tests over code that exists. **Expect the code to
be wrong in places**: the mapping never had a review of its own, and
every independent review of an area of this crate found defects that
the area's tests passed. The worst defect here is a rule table under
which two programs that differ compare equal.

## Interface

A case is a pre-image, a post-image, a rule table and an expectation:

```
tests/rules/<slug>/a.cpp        the pre-image (digested with no rule)
tests/rules/<slug>/b.cpp        the post-image (digested under the rules)
tests/rules/<slug>/rules.txt    the relocations, as text
tests/rules/<slug>/expect.txt   equal, or the units that differ, as a pair test says it
```

`rules.txt` is yours to define, at the head of `rules.rs` and in the
landed file. It names entities of the post-image and what each is to be
called; the test turns each line into the `Rule` of `spec/verify.md`
§2.1 from the unit records of the two sides (`slip_digest::records`,
which is how `slip declare` does it: `crates/slip_cli/src/declare.rs`
`resolve`). Write that resolver from the specification, not from
`declare.rs`: it is a second implementation on purpose, and one test in
`crates/slip_cli/tests/declare.rs` is to show that for three of your
cases `declare` and your resolver produce the same `RuleTable` (its
`id()`). A case may also give a rule that no declared change would
produce (a wrong one, a chained one, one that matches nothing): then the
line is the rule itself, in a second form you define.

`expect.txt` reads as digest §14.1 says for `differ` and `equal`
(`tests/common/pairs.rs` has the reader; reuse it), with one addition
you define for the counters of rule use (`spec/verify.md` §9.1).

Files you may edit: `tests/rules.rs`, `tests/rules/`, `tests/common/`
(additions only), the one test in `crates/slip_cli/tests/declare.rs`,
`spec/landed/digest/WP-103.md`, and the mapping itself where a case
shows it wrong: `RuleTable` in `crates/slip_model/src/keys.rs` and the
places of `crates/slip_digest/src/keys.rs` that call it. Other packages
are open in `keys.rs`: touch only the mapping, and merge `main` before
the gate. Anything else found wrong is reported with a pending case
(`# needs: <area>`), not fixed.

## The cases

The six of `spec/verify.md` "Tests" first: a relocated namespace, a
merged namespace, a renamed member, a swap, a collision, a rule that
captures a pre-existing entity. Then, each as its own directory:

1. **Every kind of key.** For each row of `spec/digest-kinds.md` §3.1
   that names an entity (not `resolved`, `order`, the unused 24): the
   entity inside a relocated namespace, with the rule on the namespace
   only (the entity's key maps through its parent), `equal`; and, where
   the language lets the entity be renamed, the entity itself renamed
   with its own rule, `equal`. The kinds keyed by content
   (`using_directive`, `static_assert`, `friend`, `using_enum`,
   `file_scope_asm`, `consteval_block`, `template_param_object`): what
   they mention is relocated, and their keys, which hold that content,
   still line up. Say per row which form the case takes and why a row
   has none.
2. **Discriminators.** A rule matches on (parent, kind, name, disc) with
   the discriminator already mapped. Cases: one of two overloads
   renamed, the two differing in a parameter whose type is a relocated
   class; a specialisation whose argument is a relocated class, an
   enumerator of a relocated enumeration, a pointer to a relocated
   function, a relocated template (a template template argument); a
   partial specialisation; a function template whose signature names a
   relocated concept; a member of a specialisation.
3. **Parents in mapped terms.** `a::b::c` with `a` relocated to `x` and,
   in the same table, `b` renamed: the second rule's parent is the
   mapped one. Both orders of the two lines in `rules.txt` give the same
   table. A rule whose parent is written in unmapped terms matches
   nothing, and the case shows the differences that result.
4. **Rules do not chain; a rule applies at most once.** `f` to `g` and
   `g` to `h` in one table; the swap; a rule whose target is its own
   source.
5. **Merged namespaces.** `acme` mapped onto a `legacy` that still
   exists: equal when what the two namespaces are is the same, two
   digests under one key when one is `inline` or carries an attribute
   the other lacks; a member of each with one mapped key (a collision:
   both indeterminate, never equal); nested namespaces merged at two
   levels; an unnamed namespace inside a relocated one; a namespace
   reopened in several places on one side.
6. **What maps to itself.** A function with C language linkage moved
   between namespaces with no rule (`c_linkage`: the same entity, so
   `equal`), and the same with a rule on the namespace; a builtin
   called from relocated code; the global namespace.
7. **Entities without a name** (digest §3.3): an unnamed class named
   for linkage by a typedef inside a relocated namespace; the typedef
   renamed, with and without the rule on the class. `unnamed_by` holds
   the plain name, not a mapped one (decided at WP-024's handback):
   write that sentence into `spec/verify.md` §2.1 where it belongs, and
   the case that shows it.
8. **References and types inside units.** A function outside the
   relocated namespace that calls into it, names its types, takes the
   address of its members, uses its enumerators, its templates with
   arguments from inside and outside: `equal` under the rule, and
   without the rule every such unit differs (the second half is the
   test that the first is not vacuous). Local entities of a relocated
   function; a lambda in a relocated function; an instantiation, in an
   unrelocated function, of a relocated template.
9. **Wrong and missing rules are never equal.** For five of the cases
   above: drop one rule; replace a rule's target by another existing
   name; give the rule the wrong kind; give it a discriminator of
   another overload. Each must show differences. Then try on your own
   to build a table that makes two different programs equal: two
   functions of one content exchanged while a caller changes, a rule
   that maps an entity onto one the pre-image lacks, a rule on a parent
   and a contradicting rule on a member. `spec/verify.md` §2.1 "Why
   applying rules to the post-image is sound" is the claim; every
   program pair you find equal must be an isomorphism under the table,
   and the landed file argues it for each.
10. **Counters** (`spec/verify.md` §9.1): a rule that matches nothing
    counts zero; a rule on a namespace reopened three times; an entity
    the post-image still has under its old name.
11. **Determinism under rules**: the sweep of `tests/determinism/` runs
    every case's post-image under its table twice and in two orders of
    the table's lines.

## Tests

The cases are the tests. Beside them, mutations of the mapping, each of
which a case must kill (list case and mutant in the landed file): the
parent is not mapped before matching; the discriminator is not mapped
before matching; a rule is applied to its own result; the match ignores
the kind; the match ignores the discriminator; the match is tried in
unmapped terms; merged namespaces are treated as a collision; a
collision of two functions is treated as a merge; a reference inside a
unit is written unmapped; a type digest memoised under one table is
reused under another (if nothing can reuse it, say why).

`cargo test -p slip_digest` stays under a minute once built.
